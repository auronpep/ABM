## Mission

Get a student through the MBE with **the least learning and retention humanly required.** We are not a content firehose. We do the opposite of every competitor: instead of giving you everything, we figure out the smallest set of things you actually need, and we hand you only that.

## What we actually sell

We are running comprehensive analysis across every bar question we can get, and **distilling each subject and subtopic down to the minimum a student must learn or retain to pass.** That distillation is the product. The student is paying for what we chose to leave out as much as what we put in.

We do not say this in the pitch. We also do not hide it or apologize for it. It simply isn't the marketing — it's the engine. (How to talk about the program is POSITIONING's job.)

## The belief (the 200–300 Thesis) — *internal*

There are 200 questions on the MBE. So if our product were perfect, we would have identified **200–300 distilled phrases** so precise that a ten-year-old could skip law school, run the program, read those phrases the morning of the exam, and have each one tell him exactly how to navigate the question in front of him.

That is the north star we measure against — not a promise we make to anyone. Treat it as the internal definition of "done well": the closer our key set gets to that target, the better the product is.

## What a key is (the magi principle)

Mainstream programs teach you the balanced rule: *"assault turns on whether there was a genuine apprehension of an imminent battery."* True, and nearly useless under time pressure.

We do the opposite. A key **takes a side and points at the answer.** It predicts what the question is actually testing and tells the student how to move — terse, directive, specific. The style of a key is closer to *"fake weapon → pick assault"* than to a treatise paragraph. (Illustration of the *style*, not a real key.)

This is the whole philosophy in one line: **don't explain the law, point at the answer.**

## The counterfeit principle (why keys work)

Every wrong answer is a **counterfeit** — it looks like the right answer but fails one of two tests. A correct MBE choice must be both **true** (good law) and **responsive** (it answers the exact call). Each distractor is built to fail one prong: *true but unresponsive,* or *responsive but untrue.* That is the whole game — and it is why a trained student can often read only the answer choices and still win.

## The two kinds of keys

- **Gold Key** — the *doctrinal* nugget. The narrow rule, exception, constitutional principle, magic phrase, or distinction a student is unlikely to derive, that makes a hard trap easy *once known.* A static truth. Gold settles whether a choice is **true.**
- **Silver Key** — the *mechanical* nugget. A test-taking move that gets a student **through** a hard question or a two-answer tension spot. Not a truth — a maneuver. Silver settles whether a choice is **responsive** — and the mechanical keys (reading the answer choices themselves) are the rarest edge, not the lesser tier. Silver is the sharper blade.

Gold tells you *what is true.* Silver tells you *how to move.* Full definitions live in LEXICON.
