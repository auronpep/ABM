### TEAR
Our foundational course (replaces the old C3 foundational course) and the student's core loop. One word, two readings — **rip** (what we do to the test) and **tear** (what the bar does to the unprepared); we do the tearing. Stages: **Test the answer → Expose the counterfeit → Apply the rule → Repair the pattern.**

### Test the answer / Expose the counterfeit / Apply the rule / Repair the pattern
The four TEAR stages. **Test the answer:** attempt a question, timed. **Expose the counterfeit:** name the almost-true answer for the counterfeit it is, and why it pulled. **Apply the rule:** deliver the relevant key. **Repair the pattern:** log the miss, queue reinforcement, and track the underlying pattern until it's fixed.

### Cut / Clash / Call
The in-question tactic used inside **Apply** — how a student works the answer choices on a single question. **Cut** the dead choices, find the two that **Clash**, make the **Call** with a key. (Carried from the C3 era, where it was the core navigation move; now nested under TEAR.)

### Distillation
The core process: analyzing every available bar question and reducing each subject and subtopic to the **minimum a student must learn or retain to pass.** Distillation decides what we teach. *Internal — this is the secret sauce, not the pitch.*

### Master Synergized Outline
The single outline built by ingesting thousands of resources into one synthesized source of truth for content. The backbone the distillation engine works from. *Internal.*

### Trap
A question engineered to pull a student toward a wrong pick, built around a counterfeit answer. Cataloged and prioritized by frequency.

### Counterfeit
The core principle. Every wrong answer is a counterfeit — currency that looks real but isn't, because it fails the **True & Responsive** test: it is *true but unresponsive* (a correct rule that doesn't answer the precise call) or *responsive but untrue* (it answers the call with bad law). Exposing the counterfeit is TEAR's second stage and the spine of the product — *John 7:24, "do not judge by appearances, but judge with righteous judgment."* The trap is the mechanism; the counterfeit is the tempting answer it dangles.

### True & Responsive
The answer-choice axiom that makes "read only the answer choices" possible: on a well-built MBE question the correct choice is the only one that is **both true** (legally correct) **and responsive** (it answers the exact call). Every distractor is engineered to fail one prong — and that failure is the counterfeit. Gold Keys settle whether a choice is *true*; Silver Keys settle whether it's *responsive*.

### Tension Point
A spot where a question forces a close call between two defensible answers. Where most students lose points and where Silver Keys earn their keep. Cataloged and prioritized by frequency.

### Wrong-Answer Repair
The system that turns a miss into durable learning: the wrong answer is logged, the relevant key is queued for reinforcement, and the underlying pattern is tracked until it stops recurring. The **Repair the pattern** stage of TEAR.

### Gold Key
The **doctrinal** key (a doctrinal nugget): a brief, high-value unlock for a hard MBE trap — a narrow rule, exception, constitutional principle, magic phrase, or distinction a student is unlikely to derive, that makes the question easy once known. Tells the student **what is true.**

### Silver Key
The **mechanical** key (a mechanical nugget): a brief, high-value navigation / test-taking move that gets a student through a hard question or a two-answer tension spot. Tells the student **how to move.** The mechanical keys — reading the answer choices themselves — are the rarest edge, not a lesser tier; silver is the sharper blade.

> A key **takes a side and points at the answer.** Style is terse and predictive — *"fake weapon → pick assault,"* not a balanced rule statement.

### Precision Sheet
The master distilled artifact, per subject: **overall strategy plus highly precise specifics.** The reservoir the keys are drawn from. *Largely internal; source for the personalized deliverables.*

### Customized Playbook
The personalized deliverable handed to a student later in the program. Three parts: (1) MBE-wide strategy and best practices, (2) the student's mapped weak spots, (3) per-subject **cheat sheets.**

### Cheat Sheet
A per-subject component **inside the Playbook**: the specific Gold and Silver Keys *that student* needs to lift their score. A personalized subset of the precision sheet.

### Last-Day Review Sheet
Near the end of the program: one ultra-distilled sheet **per subject** — the final, smallest set of things *that student* must keep fresh to navigate *their* weaknesses on exam day. The most personal, most distilled artifact.

### Adaptive
The program responds to the individual: it serves next steps, drills, and keys based on that student's coverage, trap/tension handling, timing, and misses.

### Guided
The student doesn't plan their own studying. The program decides. Today this means **one action — Resume.** No menus, no decision fatigue.

### The 200–300 Thesis
The internal north-star metric: if the product were perfect, ~200–300 distilled phrases would be enough for a novice to navigate all 200 MBE questions cold. A measure of "done well," **not** a marketing claim. *Internal.*

### Bounce Test
The positioning check: a neutral, conservative, non-Christian Republican should feel the site isn't for them and leave. If they're comfortable, the copy is too soft. *(See POSITIONING.)*

### Internal vs External
The engine (distillation, keys, thesis, playbook strategy) is **internal** — it's why the product works, not the pitch. We sell the outcome and don't narrate the machinery. *(See POSITIONING.)*
