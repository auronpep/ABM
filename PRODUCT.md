## TEAR — the foundational course

TEAR is our foundational course — it replaces the old C3 foundational course. The name is the metaphor: the student **rips through** the bar, tearing the counterfeit answers open instead of being caught by them.

TEAR is also the student's core loop. The four stages:

1. **Test the answer** — the student attempts a question. We time it. Speed and hesitation are signal, not just right/wrong.
2. **Expose the counterfeit** — the almost-true answer is named for what it is: a counterfeit, built to look right. The student sees *why* it pulled.
3. **Apply the rule** — the relevant **Gold Key** or **Silver Key** is delivered: the doctrinal unlock, or the move that gets them through.
4. **Repair the pattern** — the miss is logged, the key is queued for reinforcement, and the underlying pattern is tracked so the program comes back to it until it's fixed.

## Adaptive and guided — one decision

The program is **guided**: it decides what the student does next, so the student doesn't have to. Today that means there is essentially **one action — Resume.** The student presses go and the system serves the right next thing. No menus, no planning, no decision fatigue.

Modes we will add later (named now so the architecture expects them, not built yet):

- **Targeted drilling** — drill a chosen subject or question type on demand.
- **Weak-spot drills** — the system assembles drills from where the student is actually failing.
- **Lessons** — short reviews of the rules, tension points, and the specific wrong answers the student got.

Build order is honest: **Resume-guided study first.** Drilling, lessons, and the personalized deliverables below come later.

## What we track (the data spine)

Everything adaptive depends on this. For every student we track:

- **Coverage** — progress across the master outline.
- **Trap and tension handling** — how the student does on specific traps and tension areas, not just subjects.
- **Time per question** — how long they spend, where they stall.
- **Misses and repair state** — what they got wrong and whether the repair has stuck.

## The distillation engine — *internal, the secret sauce*

This is what makes the keys possible, and it is the thing the student is actually paying for (see VISION). It is not visible in the UI and not in the pitch.

- We ingest **thousands of resources** and synthesize them into **one master synergized outline.**
- We distill every subject and subtopic to the **minimum needed to pass** — distillation decides what we teach, not coverage for its own sake.
- We catalog every **tension point** and **trap**, and **prioritize them by frequency** so effort follows what the exam actually does.

The study group builds this out as it works the data. The outline and the trap/tension catalog are living internal assets.

## The keys

Keys are the atomic output of distillation. They are **unlocked as the student misses problems** — earned at the moment of failure, when they mean the most.

- **Gold Key** — the doctrinal nugget. Settles whether a choice is **true.**
- **Silver Key** — the mechanical nugget. Settles whether a choice is **responsive** — reading the answer choices themselves. The sharper blade, not the lesser tier.

Keys live inside the precision sheets below and surface in the **Apply** stage. Full definitions: LEXICON.

## The deliverables (personalized outputs)

These are distinct artifacts. They are **not** interchangeable — each has one job.

| Artifact | When | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Precision sheet** | Internal, always | The master distilled artifact per subject: overall strategy **plus** highly precise specifics. The reservoir the keys are drawn from. |
| **Customized Playbook** | Later in the program | The personalized deliverable. Three parts: (1) MBE-wide strategy and best practices, (2) the student's mapped weak spots, (3) per-subject **cheat sheets**. |
| **Cheat sheet** | Inside the Playbook | Per subject. The specific Gold and Silver Keys *that student* needs to lift their score. A personalized subset of the precision sheet. |
| **Last-Day Review Sheet** | Near the end | One ultra-distilled sheet **per subject** — the final, smallest set of things *that student* must keep fresh to navigate *their* weaknesses on exam day. |

The relationship in one line: **distillation → precision sheets → keys → (personalized) cheat sheets inside the playbook → last-day review sheet.** Each step is smaller and more personal than the last.
